Two decisions worth naming because they differ from the plan's sketch:

* **The trait takes a fetcher; an adapter cannot construct one.** `SafeFetcher` is
  the only way to the network, which is what makes §11.5's SSRF requirement
  structural rather than advisory.
* **The catalogue is registry-driven, not table-driven.** `sources` holds instance
  state (enabled, health, last checked); a source with no row must still appear in
  the catalogue, because a source nobody has imported from is still a source this
  build can read. The browser journey is what found this: on a fresh instance
  `GET /imports/sources` answered `{"items": []}` while a preview of the same
  address worked.

**The revision cache is a fetcher decorator.** `CachingFetcher` wraps
`SafeFetcher`, stores the body with the `ETag` and `Last-Modified` it arrived with,
and answers a second read from the cache when the source says `304`. The
validators are stored beside the bytes because they are validators and not secrets
(spec §10.4). Scope is the pseud's own row when a credential was used and the
public scope otherwise, so one reader's cached copy of a work they are signed in
for is not handed to another.

### `M6-02` — the adapters, four of nine tier-1 sources

| Adapter | Source shape | Fixtures | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ao3.rs` | The Archive-software family (AO3 and the same code elsewhere) | 4 pages | 7 unit + 9 fixture |
| `royalroad.rs` | One chapter per page, 109 requests for a 109-chapter work | 4 pages | 13 unit + 21 fixture |
| `syosetu.rs` | One work across several pages, 100 episodes to a list page | 12 pages | 16 unit + 31 fixture |
| `efiction.rs` | **Nineteen archives across eighteen hosts, one script** | 14 pages, 5 members | 18 unit + 37 fixture |

The eFiction adapter is this window's work and the milestone record's §7 has the
detail. Its findings in brief, each of which would have shipped as a silent
corruption:

* **The port's date table could not read either recorded member.** `%B %d, %Y`
  requires a comma and one member writes `January 18 2022`; the other writes
  `08/06/21`. Both returned no date, which becomes the epoch or the fetch time.
  Dates are now parsed by shape, day-first for the numeric form with the two
  readings disambiguated against the future.
* **A chapter's two URLs are the reading view and the *print* view, and a table of
  contents links to the print one.** `&chapter=K` links `printable.css` and fires
  `window.print()` on load. `ninelivesarchive.com` disallows
  `viewstory.php?action=printable&*` by name. The adapter reads
  `&textsize=0&chapter=K` and falls back to the other view's container.
* **A value is what the member rendered as a value.** Every class value is a
  `browse.php` link and everything else in a block is chrome — reading the chrome
  made a work's rating `Adult Download ePub`. Where values are links, the links are
  the values: that is what keeps `Male to Female, Young Adult (20-26 yrs)` whole and
  stops `Slow/Gradual Change` being split into `Slow`.
* **Archive statistics and story metadata look identical.** narutofic's
  content-gate page carries the archive's `Chapters:` and `Word count:`; read
  generically they become one work of 25,318 chapters and 47,323,633 words. The two
  are told apart structurally — container plus the `Completed:` label.
* **Three ways a page is not a story, and a fourth.** A moderation hold is a new
  `SourceError::Withheld` (terminal, and not the reader's fault); a content gate is
  `AuthRequired` and is satisfied by a credential's `adult_allowed`; a challenge
  wall is `Blocked`, matched on the interstitial's own markers; nothing at all is
  `NotFound`.
* **And one finding that was not about eFiction at all.** Every page in the family
  declares `charset=ISO-8859-1` and emits Windows-1252, and `SafeFetcher` decoded
  bodies with `String::from_utf8_lossy` — so `This week\u{fffd}s shows` was what a
  reader would have seen in their library, with nothing failing anywhere. Bodies
  are now decoded through `encoding_rs` by the declared charset, which is the
  WHATWG alias table every browser applies.

**Source reachability, measured** rather than assumed, by reading each member's
`robots.txt` and then requesting its work page: nine of eighteen are importable,
one permits a work page and forbids every chapter, two disallow the whole archive
(one of which is `tgstorytime.com`, whose markup is recorded in full), two answer
Cloudflare `403`, and four domains no longer resolve.

## What the browser journey found

The journey was run over `/import` → preview → confirm → `/library` against a
compiled binary on a seeded instance, with a Royal Road work that really is 109
chapters. Three defects, none of which a test had been asking about:

1. **The catalogue was empty on a fresh instance**, because `list_sources` read the
   `sources` table and nothing creates a row for a source nobody has imported from.
   The route is now registry-driven and `SourceAdapter` gained `display_name()`, so
   a card reads "Royal Road" rather than `royalroad`.
2. **The badge read "royal road"** — a `text-transform: lowercase` rule lowercasing
   a proper noun.
3. **The library card never said how many chapters arrived.** It now counts the
   stored chapters rather than repeating the source's claim, because the two differ
   exactly when an import is partial.

The journey also produced `ClampedText.svelte` (Royal Road's summary runs 2,400
characters and ends in Amazon/Patreon links; shown whole it turned one card into
four screens), and a fix to `describe_status`, which was printing
`answered 525 <unknown status code>` — `StatusCode`'s `Display` leaking through the
error path.

## The spec grew by three sections

Two of these came from the user mid-session and one from the ficnexus roadmap.

* **§29 — community roadmap, feature consensus, and changelog.** The mechanism
  ported from ficnexus: raw suggestions stored before interpretation, clustering
  that never rewrites a card's name, best–worst translated into all implied pairwise
  matches, the arena drawing least-compared cards first, stages and categories as
  data, and a changelog bidirectionally linked to the demand that produced it.
  Rewritten where ficnexus could not help: the trust gate is the user's own ladder
  rather than a role, and ballots are never published while ratings are.
* **§30 — non-written works, media, and aggregation.** Format becomes a property of
  every work, so a fan film is a work on the same terms as prose: same metadata,
  search, collections, comments, federation. Hosting is an *instance* setting
  (`host | reference | catalogue`), which is what lets this instance aggregate a fan
  film it does not host while another instance hosts the bytes. Embedding is our own
  shell over a provider allow-list, §8.3's "no iframes" is untouched, and completion
  is an explicit reader action rather than a player position.
* **§31 — sharing: fic cards, unfurling, and link previews.** A link to a work on
  this instance renders from internal data with **no outbound request at all**; a
  link to a known source renders from cached metadata fetched through the job queue
  under that source's own robots and pacing; every other URL is a plain link. The
  card is deterministic and identical whether a work is absent or merely unreadable
  to the caller, so it is not an existence oracle.

From the user's fifteen-item gap list, each item went to the section that owns it
rather than into an appendix: onboarding §7.8, WIP activity status §8.8, author
boundary tools §12.10, content notes §15.16, new voices §16.13 and freshness §16.14,
fandom spaces §17.12, collaborative drafting §18.8 and reading paths §18.9, AI cost
controls §22.11, federation §23.10, succession §24.11, alerting §24.12, dormancy
§24.13, cognitive accessibility §25.6.

Three things in §30/§31 are **unassigned to a milestone** and the user has not ruled
on them: media wants an importer and video adapters, so it plausibly extends M6;

The gamification section (§9.7) was also added and then reconciled in this session:

## Deliberately not done, and said so

* **`v0.07-imports` is not tagged.** The milestone is not finished; tagging it would
  make the tag a statement about intent.
* **Five tier-1 adapters are not ported**: ffnet/fictionpress, the XenForo board
  family, scribblehub, wattpad, ficbook. The first three and the `.net` boards
  answer `403` to a plain request; wattpad and ficbook are reachable and simply not
  yet recorded.
* **The Cloudflare decision is left to the user**, because it trades a documented
  security property for coverage. `SafeFetcher` resolves a hostname, checks the
  answers and pins them; a browser subprocess cannot be pinned that way and loads
  subresources nobody vetted. §9 of the milestone record lays out three options and
  recommends a sanctioned browser path behind a proxy that enforces the same
  allow-list — real work, and larger than the rest of M6's adapter work.
* **Dormant hosts stay in the eFiction allow-list.** Four of the eighteen no longer
  resolve, and the list is what the adapter claims rather than what currently
  answers, so a member that returns needs no adapter change. The consequence —
  eighteen hosts wide for one source key — is written into the adapter's own docs.
* **Author's notes are parsed and dropped** by the eFiction adapter: folding them
  into a chapter body would mean inventing markup inside prose the author wrote.
* **A partial whole-work read is never reported as success.** If a source's chapter
  list disagrees with its own stated count, the import refuses and names both
  numbers. Asserted by damaging a fixture, since neither recorded member paginates.
