# The forum, explained

The Community forum is where the people of this site talk. This page
explains every part of it in plain words.

## The basic idea

A **forum** is a collection of **topics** (conversations). Each topic is a
chain of **posts** (messages), oldest first. Anyone can read; signing in
lets you post.

Topics live in **categories**, each with a purpose:

- **General** — anything that doesn't fit elsewhere. Introduce yourself
  here.
- **Fanworks** — show off and discuss stories and art.
- **Discussion** — deeper conversation: writing craft, fandom history,
  meta.
- **Help** — questions about the site, about writing, about anything.

## Reading

Click **Community** in the top menu. You'll see the categories, and inside
each one the topics with the most recent activity at the top. A topic
title in bold means there's something new in it since you last looked.

The **search** box on the forum page searches every post on the site —
titles and message text both.

## Posting a new topic

1. Open the category your topic belongs in.
2. Click **New topic**.
3. Write a title that says what the topic is about ("Looking for beta
   reader for long fic" — not just "help").
4. Write your message and click **Post**.

## Replying

Open any topic, type in the box at the bottom, click **Reply**. Your
message appears at the end of the chain. You can edit or delete your own
posts at any time.

## Subscribing to a topic

Want to know when someone replies to a topic you care about? Click
**Subscribe** on that topic. New replies show up in your notifications, and
the topic title stays bold until you've read what's new. Click
**Unsubscribe** to stop.

## Special kinds of topics

Some topics work differently on purpose:

- **Spoilers** — a topic about a story's ending. The spoiling text is
  covered by a "spoiler" cover you click to reveal. You can't accidentally
  read the ending.
- **Reading groups** — a topic with a reading schedule: which chapters by
  which date, so a group reads together.
- **Critique circles** — a topic where authors post drafts for feedback.
  The rule of a critique circle is you give feedback to get feedback.
- **AMA** — "ask me anything": one person answers questions from everyone.

The topic's author picks the type when creating it.

## Being good to each other

The short version:

- Critique the writing, not the writer.
- Warn for spoilers — use the spoiler cover in any topic about endings.
- If someone is being a problem, use the **Report** button on their post.
  The site owner sees the report, not the person you reported.

You can also **block** an account (Account → Blocking): you stop seeing
their posts, they stop seeing yours in the forum. Blocks are silent —
they're never told.
